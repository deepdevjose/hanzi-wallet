# Colour and interaction update · 1.1

The writing studio retains its existing component positions, sizes, spacing, navigation, typography and glass surfaces. Only colour declarations changed. The language selector is the requested functional addition inside the existing Preferences dialog. A regression fixture verifies that the non-colour CSS declarations are unchanged.

| Role           | Light palette | Purpose                                |
| -------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Paper          | `#F3EFE6`     | Warm, quiet surrounding surface        |
| Writing sheet  | `#FFFDF6`     | Clear separation between ink and guide |
| Ink            | `#292C31`     | Main text and handwriting              |
| Porcelain blue | `#4E6478`     | Restrained secondary reading accent    |
| Seal red       | `#8E4038`     | Small active controls and brand mark   |
| Aged gold      | `#A27F53`     | Small progress accent                  |

The dark palette uses ink-blue charcoal, warm light text and muted terracotta accents. The broad writing area stays neutral. Dashed guides remain faint so they do not compete with the user's strokes. These are design choices; no increase in concentration has been empirically measured for this app.

## Evidence and interpretation

- The Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art describes brush, ink, paper and inkstone as the four treasures of a Chinese scholar's studio, and the use of red seal impressions: https://asia-archive.si.edu/learn/chinas-calligraphic-arts/four-treasures/
- The British Museum's Chinese ceramics guide documents monochrome ceramics, cobalt blue, and coral-red glazes: https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/large_print_guide_room_95_chinese_ceramics.pdf
- A study of 1,290 Chinese adults reports preferences involving red, cyan and blue, together with important lightness, age and gender differences: https://ir.psych.ac.cn/bitstream/311026/29772/1/Hue%2C%20chroma%2C%20and%20lightness%20preference%20in%20Chinese%20adults%20Age%20and%20gender%20differenc.pdf
- Comparative research across Japan, China and Indonesia found both shared tendencies and significant differences: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8873184/

These findings support a Chinese material reference, not a universal claim about “Asian preferences.” Warm paper and dark ink are a task-oriented interpretation; restrained saturation and sparse red accents are intended to reduce visual competition. Preference studies do not prove that a palette improves handwriting concentration.
